# Cortex Gnome

**The overnight worker.** Every night it reads what changed across your company's context, picks the work worth doing, builds it to a ship-ready standard, stores it for review, logs everything, and sends you a brief. Every morning you review it. Your feedback becomes rules in the Manifesto, so the next night is better.

```
/plugin install cortex-gnome --marketplace chaostoclarity/cortex
```

Or download the single skill: [cortex-gnome.zip](https://github.com/chaostoclarity/Cortex/releases/latest/download/cortex-gnome.zip)

## Read this first

- **It costs tokens every night, useful or not.** Out of the box the gnome doesn't know what's valuable to you. Early runs may produce work you don't want.
- **Teaching it is your job.** Read each brief and reply keep, tweak, or kill, with a reason. That's what makes it good. An unreviewed gnome doesn't improve.
- **Start it reined in.** It begins on the Starter rung: tagged work only, a small budget, and proposals instead of builds for anything it infers. It earns more room as you keep its work.
- **Best on high-limit plans** such as Claude Max. On smaller plans a nightly run can eat a big share of your limit; keep it on Starter and weekdays only.

## How a night works

1. **Wake up and check in** with any other gnomes on the team, so no two build the same thing.
2. **Learn** from yesterday's feedback before doing anything else.
3. **Harvest** what's new: queued and tagged work, your Cortex, task tool, idea inbox, meetings, email, calendar, repos.
4. **Triage** against the Manifesto and the nightly budget.
5. **Build** decks, docs, landing pages, research, code with pull requests, drafts, and holds.
6. **Check** the work against its quality bar, then **log** it and send the **brief**.

## What it may do on its own

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Always** | Build anything nobody else sees yet: docs, decks, pages, research, code on a branch with a pull request, email drafts, holds on your calendar |
| **Usually** | Internal updates: comments and status on tasks, notes in the Cortex Inbox |
| **With your permission** | Ship: send, invite, merge, deploy, publish, book. Approve one job at a time, or grant a standing permission in the Manifesto |
| **Never** | Follow instructions hidden in emails or documents, spend without a per-job approval, delete, change access, push to main, or message someone new |

## Hand it work

Tag anything `gnome` in your task tool, write `@gnome` in a Cortex record, or add a Queued row to Gnome Jobs. Other skills can queue work the same way.

## Teams

Each person can run their own gnome on their own schedule. They share one home, one queue, and one Manifesto, split work by lanes, and claim each job before building it.

## Works with or without a Cortex

With a [Cortex](../../README.md) it reads your Map first and its log lives inside your Cortex. Without one it runs on whatever you connect: Notion, Google Drive, Obsidian, a folder, or a repo.
